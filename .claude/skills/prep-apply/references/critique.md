# Critiquing the drafts

Write `critique.md` into the run directory. Short - a screenful. You are reading
as the person who will receive this application, not as the person who wrote it.

The reader is a hiring manager with forty applications and four minutes.

## What to say

**The CV.** Does the first screenful answer the job? Is the strongest evidence
in it, or is it at bullet nine? Name the weakest bullet and why - a bullet with
no figure, a bullet describing a duty rather than an outcome, a bullet that
could appear on anyone's CV. Say what to cut: a tailored CV is shorter than the
one it came from, and if nothing was cut, nothing was tailored.

**The letter.** Quote the worst sentence back. Almost always it is the opening,
and almost always it is generic because it could have been written from the job
title alone. A letter that does not name something specific about the work is a
letter nobody finishes.

**The gaps.** `fit-after.md` lists what the posting asks for that the profile
cannot back. Say plainly whether those are fatal for this application or worth
applying anyway, and if there is an honest way to address one in the letter -
adjacent experience, a demonstrated ability to pick things up - say so. Never
suggest claiming it.

## What not to do

- Do not praise. A critique that opens with what is strong is a critique nobody
  reads to the end.
- Do not rewrite. Say what is wrong and let them decide.
- Do not soften a real problem. If this application is weak, the useful thing is
  to say so while they can still spend the hour elsewhere.
